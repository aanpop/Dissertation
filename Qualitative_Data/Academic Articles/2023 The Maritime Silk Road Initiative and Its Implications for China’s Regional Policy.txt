ABSTRACT

The competition between the United States and China has intensified since
the Trump administration, and the two sides have clashed in the areas
such as the economy, military, politics, technology, and human rights. This
conflict presents countries in the Indo-Pacific region with the challenge of
navigating foreign policy with these two hegemonic powers. The
Philippines is one such country facing the challenge of maintaining a balanced relationship with the two countries as a major concern for a strategic triangle. The incumbent Philippine president must calculate the
benefits and risks before formulating policies. Facing such a dilemma, former President Aquino chose to file a lawsuit with the International
Arbitration Court and strengthen cooperation with the United States to
defend the sovereignty of the Philippines in the SCS, whereas former
President Duterte chose to shelve the sovereignty of the SCS and to
cooperate with China under the framework of the BRI. However, President
Duterte quickly changed his anti-United States stance in the climate of the
ongoing conflict between the Philippines and China over islands and reefs
in the SCS and then cooperated with the United States to reinstate the
military cooperation agreement that he had suspended. Current President
Marcos Jr. has adopted a more subtle approach. He declared that he
would maintain economic cooperation with China and, at the same time,
would strengthen bilateral cooperation with the United States, thereby
attempting to maintain a balanced relationship with the two countries. In
sum, the Philippines has adopted a dynamic balancing strategy to defend
its national interests in the face of the strategic competition between the
United States and China in the Indo-Pacific region.

1. Introduction
Under China’s rapid economic development, many believed that the country would integrate
more deeply into international organizations such as the World Trade Organization (WTO) and
United Nations (UN) and that the Chinese political and economic system would liberalize and
democratize when the country was embedded in the US policymaking community (Economy,
2019, p. 42). However, when Xi Jinping assumed office, he adopted a different policy than
China’s previous leaders, imposing a more authoritarian leadership. In terms of internal affairs,
Xi strengthened his control over the country, and Chinese people’s freedom of speech was
severely restricted by the Chinese government. In terms of technology, Xi is prompting the

Chinese–US relations dramatically changed under the presidency of Donald Trump, who was

Trump administration took decisive action and implemented policies promoting free and fair

the US government vied for support from the governments of these countries.
As Medeiros stated, “China is rising and challenging US interest at the same time that Asia as
a continent is increasing in long-term importance to the United States and the world” (2019, p.

between China and the United States. A medium-sized country in this region, the Philippines,
and United States have had close relations since the Philippines’ independence. The United States
is not only a key economic partner of the Philippines but also a critical supporter of its national
defense and security. However, China is also actively ingratiating itself with the Philippines in the
hope that they can cooperate under the BRI framework at a time when the Philippines is
attempting to attract foreign direct investment to boost its economy. The main purpose of this
study was to explore how the Philippines as a medium-sized country pursues its national interests
and development strategies in the context of the competition between the United States and
China. This article will apply a strategic triangle of China, the United States, and the Philippines
as a research framework, conducted by Professor Soong as shown in the introduction chapter of
this issue, to analyze how the Philippines interacts with China and the United States. In Section
2, this article will mainly analyze relations in economy, security, technology, and politics between
the United States and China from Trump to Biden administration. Next, this article will analyze
triangle relations in economy, security, and politics between the Philippines, China, and the
United States in the following sections. Besides, this article will also apply a neoclassic realism
view, which is a comprehensive and helpful theory to international relations analysis as a research
approach to probe how the Philippines responds USA–China power rivalry in the IndoPacific region.

1990, China spent less than 4% of what the United States spent on its military. By 2000, China’s
defense budget was only 8% that of the United States and no more than 17% that of the United

project that China will surpass the United States within the next decade to become the world’s

are mainly sold to the United States. In addition, US companies invest in China, enabling
Chinese companies to establish factories to produce products and sell them back to the
United States.
After Chinese President Xi assumed office, he proposed the “Chinese dream” of the successful
rejuvenation of the Chinese nation. Therefore, the Chinese government has proposed a series of
national development plans to realize the Chinese dream. The Made in China 2025 strategy is
one such plan and industrial policy of the Chinese Communist Party intended to advance the
Chinese manufacturing sector. Such policies aim to make China the dominant global leader in
global high-tech manufacturing through government subsidies, mobilization of state-owned enterprises, and the pursuit of intellectual property rights, eventually surpassing Western countries in
advanced industrial sectors (McBride & Chatzky, 2019).
In terms of diplomacy, the Chinese government advances its national interest in the region
through the grand strategy of the BRI. Xi launched the BRI, formerly known as One Belt One
Road, to promote economic prosperity and regional economic cooperation in 2013. The BRI aims
to connect Asia to Europe and Africa through the Silk Road Economic Belt and 21st Century
Maritime Silk Road (Chao, 2021, p. 48). The BRI prioritizes infrastructure and connectivity to
promote economic cooperation and integration with the Association of Southeast Asian Nations
(ASEAN) and Central Asian countries in areas such as trade, investment, transportation, information, and people-to-people exchange (Soong, 2018, p. 292). China’s BRI is one of the most ambitious infrastructure projects ever conceived. The project is often viewed as an unsettling extension
of China’s rising power and a dramatic expansion of China’s economic and political influence.
Some skeptics are concerned that China is laying a debt trap for borrowing governments. The

2.2. The US response to a rising China
China’s comprehensive development, population size, and growth potential all contribute to its
status as a world power with the ability to challenge the United States’ status and national interest
as a leading global power in the coming era (Graaff & Apeldoorn, 2018, p. 113). The Chinese
government’s actions and development plans have elevated the United States’ concern that China
is growing in influence and becoming a revisionist state. Trump drastically changed the foreign
strategy of his predecessor, Barack Obama, with “America First” adopted as the core doctrine of
US foreign policy under the Trump administration. These actions resulted in a major shift in
Chinese–US relations, with many guidelines of Chinese and US policy, such as engagement,
cooperation, and convergence, called into question (Medeiros, 2019, p. 93). To “make America
great again” and stimulate the growth of the US economy, Trump launched a trade war with
China under the America First strategy. In the beginning, the Trump administration pressured
China to revise its unfair trade practices against the United States by imposing tariffs on
imported Chinese goods. When China rejected the request, Trump further raised tariffs from
10% to 25% on Chinese imports worth US$200 billion in the summer of 2019. In June 2020,
Trump imposed visa restrictions on Chinese students and scholars associated with the Chinese
government in the belief that China used them to illegally acquire US intellectual property
(Boylan et al., 2021, pp. 23–25). The conflict between the two sides spread from the economy
and trade to competition in areas such as the military, diplomacy, and science and technology.
The United States also proposed an Indo-Pacific initiative in response to the challenges generated through the BRI. Xi’s BRI is viewed by many experts, analysts, and politicians in the United
States as means to leverage economic statecraft to impose a military presence and increase diplomatic influence in the region (Medeiros, 2019, p. 99). As a 2019 Asia Society Task Force report
notes, “Chinese investments in ports in Burma, Sri Lanka, and Pakistan, and a naval base in
Djibouti, as well as large infrastructure investment projects in Africa, Latin America, Europe, and
the Pacific Island nations, appear to be parts of a long-term plan to extend Chinese military
access beyond Asia to the rest of the world” (Schell & Shirk, 2019, p. 27). The Indo-Pacific strategy was proposed by former US President Trump during his visit to Hanoi, Vietnam, to attend
the APEC summit in November 2017. Subsequently, the White House released the National
Security Strategy 2017, formally employing the “Indo-Pacific” regional concept to replace the previously used terms “Asia and the Pacific” or “Asia-Pacific.” The Indo-Pacific strategy became the
main axis of the Trump administration’s Asian policy, with China as the main target of
the strategy.

Under the Indo-Pacific strategy, the United States actively participates in and leads the
Quadrilateral Security Dialogue (QUAD) among the United States, Japan, India, and Australia. At
the same time, the United States, United Kingdom, and Australia (called AUKUS) announced a
security pact strengthening military capabilities in the Indo-Pacific region in response to Chinese
influence and expansion. US policymakers maintain that foundational technology is essential to
future innovation, productivity, and national security and is pivotal to controlling the global
economy in the 21st century.
The United States viewed the Made in China 2025 strategy as contradicting China’s stated
adherence to international trade rules as well as posing a security risk. The US government argues
that the plan encompasses the discriminatory treatment of foreign investment, forced technology
transfers, intellectual property theft, and cyber espionage (McBride & Chatzky, 2019).
Consequently, the United States has begun to restrict Chinese companies’ access to high-tech
technologies and products and has used tariffs and other tools to encourage US high-tech companies to move their supply chains out of China (Medeiros, 2019, pp. 100–101).
The strategic competition between the two major powers, the United States and China, poses
challenges for countries in the Indo-Pacific region. Most small and medium-sized countries in
this region have close relations with the United States and China and must determine how to
skillfully interact with these two competing nations. The following sections explore how the
Philippines, a medium-sized country, has responded to the competition between the United
States and China.

3. The political and security strategies of the Philippines toward China and the
United States
The Philippines must not only maintain a strategic balance between China and the United States
but must also take its territorial conflict with China in the South China Sea (SCS) into account.
The greatest sources of conflict and threats to security and politics for the Philippines are the
Abu Sayyaf Islamic terrorist group and the sovereignty dispute with China over islands and reefs
in the SCS. Although relations between China and the Philippines improved between the mid1990s and the early 2000s, as De Castro (2007, p. 7) stated, “Manila will realize that a relationship
based on realpolitik is more fragile than that driven by shared values and ideologies.” The conflict
between the Philippines and China in the SCS ultimately led to a deadlock in their diplomatic
relationship. Therefore, in view of China’s strict stance on the sovereignty of the SCS and the
conflict between the two sides over Scarborough Shoal, the Aquino administration under
President Benigno Aquino III in the Philippines strengthened its cooperation with the United
States to counteract the pressure from China.
Although the Philippines chose to cooperate with the United States to resist pressure from
China generated through current sovereignty disputes over islands and reefs in the SCS, military
cooperation between the Philippines and United States has a long history. The Philippines
allowed for the establishment of the US bases of Clark Field (now called Clark Air Base) air force
base and Subic Bay strategic naval base during the Cold War, resulting in the United States
becoming the second-leading employer in the Philippines (Woods, 2016, p. 161). The Philippines
signed a visiting forces agreement, a mutual logistics support agreement, to bolster the
Philippine–US military relationship with the United States in 1997. Furthermore, the Philippines
has designated a major non-NATO ally in 2003. The Philippines also held regular military exercises with the United States and worked to deepen US interest in the Philippines’ security by
assigning the United States a role in the reform of the Philippine military, including training
Filipino troops (Roy, 2005, p. 314). Therefore, for the Philippines, cooperation with the United
States is based on strategic consideration and historical factors. The Philippine government is also
highly aware that the country cannot stand alone against China in terms of overall national

strength. Therefore, the Aquino administration adopted the strategy of introducing the strength
of another powerful country, namely the United States, to jointly resist China’s influence.
Consequently, Aquino actively cooperated with the United States in military affairs.
The former Aquino administration of the Philippines also took legal steps to defend its sovereignty in the SCS. Unable to resolve the competing claims of China through diplomatic negotiations, the Philippines turned to international law. The former Aquino administration initiated
compulsory arbitrations against China in the hope that a UN tribunal would reject China’s claims
of sovereignty over the SCS and nine-dash line (Jones, 2016, pp. 75–76). The Philippines won its
arbitration case against China after the Permanent Court of Arbitration in The Hague,
Netherlands, ruled that China’s claim to the nine-dash line in these disputed waters was invalid
in 2016.
The strategy toward China underwent a major shift when Rodrigo Duterte assumed the presidency. Duterte is a mercurial, inflammatory character and often makes off-the-cuff public statements (Dalpino et al., 2016, p. 5). He reversed the Philippines’ previous strategic decision of
aligning with the United States against China. He tried to ease diplomatic and security tensions
with China over the SCS arbitration. He announced that he would not be declaring war with
China over the SCS dispute and expressed his hope that China could assist the Philippines with
the Build, Build, Build infrastructure program launched by his administration (Tai & Huang,
2018, p. 357). To break the deadlock, Duterte designated former President Fidel V. Ramos as a
special envoy to China to express the Philippines’ intention to reestablish stable relations between
the two nations. Ramos successfully broke the bilateral stalemate, and Duterte undertook an official visit to China. Responding positively to the actions of Duterte, the Chinese government lifted
its restrictions on the import of Philippine fruit, which has been imposed since the conflict over
Scarborough Shoal and Huangyan Island in 2012 (Hsu, 2015, p. 21).
Duterte also changed the Philippines’ US policy because of his background, experience, and
view that the United States is an unreliable ally as well as in response to the United States’ criticism of human rights violations in the Philippine drug war. He also believed that the United
States had failed to prevent China from constructing artificial islands in the SCS—an act that
threatened the Philippines’ sovereignty in the region (Parameswaran, 2016). Duterte declared that
he would never visit the United States after a US Congress hearing about the human rights violations committed under his deadly antidrug campaign. Moreover, he decided to terminate the 21year-old visiting forces agreement when the visas of a Filipino senator and the police chief who
oversaw the Philippine drug war were canceled (Aspinwall, 2020).
Although Duterte supported Bongbong Marcos Jr. during his presidential campaign, when
Marcos Jr. became president, he amended Duterte’s foreign policy toward China and United
States. During his campaign, he declared that “he would maintain the nation’s alliance with the
United States and tread carefully with Beijing over an SCS territorial dispute. Military deals
between the United States and the Philippines are advantageous to both countries. The alliance
between the two nations is a special relationship, and the United States can do many things to
help the Philippines. However, the Philippines should carefully navigate its tensions with China.
You have to be friends with everyone. You do not want to be enemies with anyone” (Calonzo,
2022). The new president of the Philippines is pursuing a strategy of maintaining stable relations
with both the United States and China. He wishes to avoid a conflict with China over the SCS
dispute; however, he does not wish to follow Duterte’s policy of hostility toward the United
States. He hopes to maintain close relations with the United States in terms of security and military cooperation for the national interests of the Philippines.
According to the aforementioned analysis, although navigating strategic competition between
the United States and China and the conflict with China over the SCS reefs, Duterte changed the
previous president’s pro-United States stance in terms of security and political strategy. He
shelved disputes with China and pursued economic cooperation instead. On the basis of his

4. The economic cooperation of the Philippines in the context of the United States
and China
Traditionally, the Philippines has had close relations with the United States concerning the economy, culture, military, and society since the Philippines’ independence. In terms of national economy, the Philippines has strong trade and investment relations with the United States. The
United States is the Philippines’ third-largest trading partner and is one of the largest foreign
investors in the Philippines. The two countries signed a bilateral Trade and Investment
Framework Agreement in 1989 and signed agreements on customs administration and trade
facilitation in 2010.
The United States also provided over US$4.5 billion in assistance over the past 20 years (US
Department of State, 2022). According to the Philippine Department of Foreign Affairs, the
United States, through the United States Agency for International Development (USAID), is a
longstanding development partner of the Philippines. The USAID has provided various programs
and projects in the Philippines that accelerated broad-based, inclusive economic growth, with a
focus on education, family health care, democracy and governance, sustainable environmental
management, and peace and stability in Mindanao (Philippine Department of Foreign Affairs,
2022). These actions reflect the close relationship between the United States and the Philippines
in terms of economic cooperation and national development. Despite Duterte’s pro-China foreign
policy, the Philippines has continued to work closely with the United States.
Regarding China’s BRI, the Philippines was initially unenthusiastic about participating in the
BRI proposed by Chinese President Xi in 2013 because of the SCS sovereignty disputation with
China (Chao, 2021, p. 48). After Duterte became president in 2016, he emphasized the critical
nature of economic cooperation with China and maximizing the Philippines’ economic interests
and trade benefits by rebuilding a friendly relationship with China (Hsu, 2015, p. 20). Duterte
decided to de-escalate the SCS dispute and declared that the Philippines would participate in
China’s BRI to attract Chinese investment and empower the Philippines, particularly in terms of
infrastructural development (Soong, 2018, p. 297). Thus, Duterte’s strengthening of ties with
China was accompanied by an increase in investment linked to the BRI.
Duterte also paid a state visit to China shortly after taking office. During Duterte’s official visit
in 2016, both countries signed company-to-company deals worth US$15 billion, and 9 billion US
dollars of loans were pledged by Chinese banks either for specific projects or to Philippine firms.
Seventeen memoranda of understanding and other deals were signed covering a wide array of
projects in sectors ranging from transportation and manufacturing to renewable energy. Duterte’s
pro-China policy enabled him to quickly obtain economic benefits from China. However, in the
5 years since then, these projects have had a slow start. In 2020, of the US$4.6 billion set aside for
current projects, China provided US$620 million in ODA to the Philippines, most of which is still
in the procurement phase (Hart, 2021).
President Marcos Jr. hopes to continue Duterte’s policy of maintaining friendly relations and
engaging in economic cooperation with China as well as of attracting Chinese investment in the
Philippines to promote its economic and infrastructure development. As mentioned, Marcos Jr.
does not wish to drastically change Duterte’s China policy but also does not wish to maintain
Duterte’s anti-United States stance; instead, he is pursuing the maintenance of friendly relations
with both countries at the same time (Lema, 2022). Therefore, under the Marcos Jr.

5. The strategic choices of the Philippines under the China–United States
power rivalry
The optimal strategy for a small- and medium-sized country navigating conflicts between two
super powers is the maintenance of friendly relations with both countries rather than favoring
one over the other and thus generating conflict with the marginalized super power. Therefore, in
the context of the strategic competition and conflict between the United States and China, the
Philippines has adopted a foreign policy of balancing relations with both countries, despite the
challenges of such an approach.
Because the United States has never exhibited a willingness to take concrete action to assist
the Philippines in its conflict with China, the Philippines has suspended its competition for sovereignty in the SCS in favor of maintaining friendly relations with China in exchange for the economic benefits of Chinese investment in the Philippines. In addition, in terms of relations with
the United States and in view of their longstanding cooperation, the Philippines must maintain
close relations with the United States. The Philippines can thus obtain US assistance and economic resources and may also use the United States as a bargaining chip to strengthen its power
in negotiations with China over the SCS conflict.
For the Philippines, a second strategic choice is to take the situation of the sovereignty conflict
with China in the SCS into consideration in the formulation of its foreign policy strategy. China’s
expansion-based actions in the SCS threaten the sovereignty and national interests of the
Philippines but the two countries are ill-matched in terms of national strength. The Philippines
must choose to cooperate with and seek assistance from the United States. Although the United
States has not been willing to take action to assist the Philippines in the SCS conflict, the
Philippines can take advantage of the strategic competition between the United States and China
to leverage the United States into supporting the Philippines’ actions in the SCS. Moreover, the
Philippines can use the United States to strengthen its military capability to prepare for potential
threats from China.
The least effective strategic option for the Philippines would be to exacerbate the conflict with
China in the SCS and weaken their relationship with the United States, placing the Philippines in
a disadvantageous situation. The Philippines cannot afford a war against China alone over the
SCS conflict without US assistance. Moreover, in view of the Philippines and the United States’
longstanding cooperation in many fields, souring relations with the United States in the midst of
a conflict with China would be an unwise strategy.
The Philippines employs the two strategies of engagement and hedging under the
China–United States power rivalry. The hedging strategy is maintaining a modest level of defense
cooperation with the United States. As Roy stated, “The Philippines practices open but low-intensity balancing with the United States against China. Manila clearly perceives an external security
threat in growing Chinese power” (Roy, 2005, pp. 305, 314). The Philippines bolsters its weak
military power relative to China’s to defend its SCS claim by maintaining favorable relations with
the United States.
However, balancing relations with the United States does not prevent the Philippines from
maintaining an economic cooperation policy with China (Roy, 2005, p. 314). Duterte had hoped
to connect his Build, Build, Build program with China’s BRI by setting aside the sovereignty dispute with China in the SCS and participating in China’s BRI to benefit national economic
growth. China responded positively to these actions, committing to invest in the Philippines and
proposing many bilateral economic cooperation plans.

However, although Duterte is a long-time anti-United States agitator and withdrew from the
visiting forces agreement with the US military, he quickly changed his attitude toward the United
States and decided to resume the suspended agreement. He took this action in light of the continuing conflict with China in the SCS, and because he perceived China as failing to fulfill its
commitments to the Philippines. In March and April 2021, more than 220 Chinese ships were
anchored on a reef disputed by the two countries, further angering Filipino officials and Duterte,
reigniting the broader maritime sovereignty dispute between China and the Philippines. After
18 months of threats, Duterte decided to revive the visiting forces agreement which originally he
planned to suspend the agreement in February 2020 with the US military (Jennings, 2021).
In sum, Duterte tactfully used an engagement and balancing strategy. The Philippines thus
uses the competitive relationship between the United States and China to benefit its national
interests. The Philippines participated in China’s BRI to obtain economic benefits and, when concerned about the conflict with China in the SCS, turned to the United States for assistance to
defend the Philippines’ sovereignty. All these policy choices in politics, economy, and security by

Poor infrastructure in the Philippines has limited its economic development. Therefore, the
Philippines requires Chinese investment to fund the construction of infrastructure and economic

for developing relations with China. China is promoting the BRI and is actively assisting with the
infrastructural development of the countries involved. Such an initiative may meet the needs of
the Philippines.
The incumbent Philippine president must calculate the benefits and risks before formulating
policies. Facing such a dilemma, former President Aquino chose to file a lawsuit with the
International Arbitration Court and strengthen cooperation with the United States to defend
the sovereignty of the Philippines in the SCS, whereas former President Duterte chose to shelve
the sovereignty of the SCS and cooperate with China under the framework of the BRI. However,
President Duterte quickly changed his anti-United States stance in the climate of the ongoing
conflict between the Philippines and China over islands and reefs in the SCS and then cooperated
with the United States to reinstate the military cooperation agreement that he had suspended.
Current President Marcos Jr. has adopted a more subtle approach. He declared that he would
maintain economic cooperation with China, and at the same time, would strengthen bilateral
cooperation with the United States, thereby attempting to maintain a balanced relationship with
the two countries. All these actions model of the Philippines could be classified as Model D,

this article, we also made a table to summarize the tendency of the Philippines’ hedging strategy
application and operation (see Table 2). Before 2016, the Philippines had closer relations with the
United States in political, economic, and security dimensions than China. So we give higher
points in all political, economic, and security dimensions for the Philippines’ strategy toward the
United States than China. However, President Duterte changed the Philippines’ strategy toward
the United States and China from 2016 to 2022. He adopted pro-China strategy in the economic
dimension and tried to shelve the sovereignty of the SCS with China. Therefore, there are higher
points in all political, economic, and security dimension for the Philippines’ strategy toward
China than the United States in the period of 2016 to 2022. However, we give higher points in all
dimensions for Philippine strategy toward the United States than China when Current President
Marcos Jr. decides to adopt a more subtle approach from 2022 to the present. He not only tries
to maintain economic cooperation relations with China as what formal President Duterte did but
also restores traditional close relations with the United States. In sum, the Philippines has
adopted a dynamic hedging strategy to defend its national interests in the face of the strategic
competition between the United States and China in the Indo-Pacific region.
