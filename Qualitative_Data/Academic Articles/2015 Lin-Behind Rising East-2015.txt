Behind Rising East Asian Maritime Tensions with China
Struggle without Breaking
ABSTRACT

Rising East Asian maritime tensions between China and its neighbors since 2009
without military conflict are explained by compelling factors including China’s
assertiveness, the expanding US regional presence, territorial defiance by China’s
neighbors, and restraining factors including Beijing’s ‘‘struggle without breaking’’
policy guideline, the US accommodation school, and neighbors’ hedging strategy.
K E Y W O R D S : China, Japan, Diaoyus/Senkakus, South China Sea, US

Restraining factors derive from the rising economic interdependence

To begin with, a review of the Sino–Japanese tension in the East China Sea
in 2012 may shed light on the key parameters of the analysis.

THE 2012 SINO–JAPANESE SENKAKU-DIAOYU ISLANDS CRISIS

On April 16, 2012, Tokyo Mayor Ishihara Shintaro announced his intention
to purchase for his city the Senkaku Islands—also claimed by China and
Taiwan as the Diaoyu Islands. The islands were owned at the time by the
Kurihara family, private citizens of Japan. The move escalated tension
between China and Japan which did not subside until the year’s end, though
temporarily. Being an ally of Japan, the United States played a prominent role
in the unfolding Sino–Japanese contention. During the crisis, more than
a few observers—including US Secretary of Defense Leon Panetta—feared
an eruption of open military conﬂict.4
During this period of heightened tension, the behaviors of all three actors
exhibited a certain degree of duality, where words and deeds of one actor over
time were alternately combative and conciliatory, in seeming selfcontradiction. Consequently, tensions rose but never spilled over into violent
confrontation. The following highlights of events illustrate the ambivalent
behavior of these three actors.
The Japanese Prime Minister, Noda Yoshihiko, held island seizure exercises with the United States in late August. However, for fear of antagonizing
Beijing, he twice turned down Mayor Ishihara’s request to land on the
Senkaku/Diaoyu islands. Noda purchased the islands for the nation on

COMPELLING FACTORS: EXTERNAL TOUGHNESS YIELDS
INTERNAL RETURNS

It is nothing new in international relations when the government takes
a tough stand toward foreign countries which caters to the domestic audience
to gain desirable domestic effects. Simply put, external toughness yields
internal returns. During this period of rising East Asian maritime tension,
such behaviors have been markedly more common.
In the current context, two observations merit attention. First, this state
behavior contrasts distinctly with that in the past when outward toughness of
a state, besides addressing a domestic audience, might also harbinger military
actions abroad, which has not happened recently in East Asia. Second,
national sentiments, rather than an autonomous and bottom-up force unbridled by top leadership, have mostly been manipulated and exploited, yet still
controlled, by ambitious power elites as a tool for their own political ends.15
In general, the intended effects of a tough act toward foreign countries
could divert attention from domestic problems, vent domestic popular frustration, reduce domestic pressure on a leadership being criticized for being
weak in foreign policy,16 and draw domestic applause to gain media coverage,

Broadly speaking, these desired effects fall into two categories: to reduce
domestic losses, and to enhance domestic gains.
A typical example of loss reduction occurred on August 10, 2012, when
South Korean President Lee Myung-bak made an unprecedented visit to
disputed Dokdo Island, which Japan had claimed for decades and called
Takeshima. At the time, his popularity was at an all-time low due to a bribery
scandal involving his brother. His act was widely perceived as a rescue for his
declining political standing. Tension between Tokyo and Seoul mounted and
persisted for months before dying down.
An example of gain enhancement is the April 2012 announcement of
Mayor Ishihara regarding purchasing the Senkakus. As later events manifested, this act which reverberated externally contained a heavy dose of
domestic political calculus. In September, his son, Ishihara Nobuteru,
entered the race for the chairmanship of the opposition (LDP) party. When
that failed, Ishihara decided in late October to quit his Tokyo mayoralty and
enter parliamentary elections himself, aiming for national leadership.
Another example is Beijing’s announcement in November 2013 of establishing an ‘‘air defense identiﬁcation zone’’ facing Japan in the East China Sea
shortly after China President Xi Jinping had promulgated an ambitious
60-item reform project. Xi’s tough act toward Japan won him enthusiastic
support from the Chinese populace,17 which Xi would need for overcoming
the resistance to his reforms from the vested interests who felt threatened.
In the following sections, the behaviors of China, the United States, and
China’s neighbors during the recent period of rising East Asian maritime
tension are examined in light of how a state’s outward toughness originates
from domestic political dynamics.

First, the departure of Zeng Qinghong from Beijing’s policymaking center. It deprived Hu Jintao of a capable ‘‘point man’’ in handling crises. Before
Zeng quit the Secretariat of the Central Committee in November 2007, he

academic visitors to the United States spread such impressions at home,
which inﬂated the national pride. A sentiment that a rising China should
no longer tolerate ‘‘territorial provocations’’ from the neighbors prevailed,
and therefore put pressure on the Beijing leadership to behave assertively.

CHINA’S NEIGHBORS’ TERRITORIAL DEFIANCE

After 2009, East Asian states with maritime territorial disputes with China—
Japan, the Philippines, and Vietnam—became observably more deﬁant
toward Beijing than before as they were threatened by China’s growing
assertiveness and emboldened by the heightened US proﬁle in East Asia. The
territorial deﬁance of Vietnam and the Philippines might have been driven by
domestic power dynamics in each country where leaders tapped nationalism
as a ready source of political energy. However, on top of all that, the two
countries’ territorial deﬁance was also indirectly triggered by domestic political contentions inside China and the United States, resulting in China’s
assertiveness and the escalated role of the United States as a supporter of the
smaller threatened states. For Japan, however, its own domestic dynamics—
personiﬁed by former Tokyo Mayor Ishihara—came into play in a regional
environment made already more volatile by the domestic political rivalry in
China and the United States.
What compelled the East Asian maritime tension to rise in recent years
were mainly three factors: China’s post-2008 assertiveness, the expanding US
East Asian presence, and China’s neighbors’ territorial deﬁance. All three had
domestic origins in one way or another. However, equally salient but often
ignored are factors that restrained the rising East Asian maritime tension from
deteriorating into warﬁghting. The common theme that underlies the
restraining factors is the rising economic interdependence among all East
Asian actors. Even in the tension between South Korea and Japan, shared
economic interests played a restraining role.

THE RISING US ACCOMMODATION SCHOOL

Midway into the ﬁrst decade of the new millennium, a school of thought
budded in the United States advocating that the country adjust, adapt, or
accommodate itself to China, because the latter’s rise was unstoppable. To
a growing number of American opinion leaders—even conservatives who
used to advocate containing the PRC—China became one ‘‘we can’t contain
and can’t afford to ﬁght,’’ as judged in 2005 by James Pinkerton, who had
served in the White House under Presidents Ronald Reagan and George

academic writings and ofﬁcial language, and gradually began to affect how
Washington conducted its foreign policy, though the declared China policy
remained unchanged.

cannot be ignored. It differs from previous US options on China policy,
such as containment, engagement, and even ‘‘congagement,’’ in the role of

CONCEPTUALIZATION AND CONCLUSIONS

This study on the rising East Asian maritime tensions with China in recent
years has produced the following observations.
1. Imminent open military conﬂicts erupting from East Asian maritime
tensions with China, as expected by many, have so far not happened.
Popular perceptions tend to overlook a more complicated reality,
thanks to the media’s proclivity for highlighting contention rather than
consultations and compromises.
2. Rising East Asian maritime tensions with China have stemmed from
a set of compelling factors: China’s post-2008 assertiveness, the United
States expanding its presence in East Asia, and China’s neighbors’
territorial deﬁance. All three foreign policy behaviors have a domestic
root: An actor’s external toughness yields internal returns, or toughness
abroad draws domestic applause (see ﬁgure 1).
3. These tensions have not spilled over into armed clashes thanks to a set
of restraining factors: ‘‘struggle without breaking,’’ which is Beijing’s
decades-long foreign policy guideline; growing accommodationist
thinking in the United States; and China’s neighbors’ adopting a hedging strategy. All three reﬂect the rising economic interdependence
among regional powers and blocs such as ASEAN. The economic
dependence of its neighbors on China restrains them from launching
war against it. Economic interdependence between China and the
United States, on the other hand, not only restrains both countries
from entering into military conﬂict against each other but also restrains
China from launching war on its neighbors, which would disrupt
China–US economic relations and damage China’s economy.
4. Compelling factors are often timed with periodic leadership transitions
of major powers, as domestic power contenders need to act tough to
win support. Restraining factors, however, belong to a persistent trend.
Economic interdependence will only grow with time. Comparatively

Comparing the two theaters of the East China Sea and South China
Sea, the study ﬁnds some differences. In the former, China’s behavior

‘‘Struggle without breaking,’’ though of Chinese origin, best summarizes the dialectical behaviors of all key actors under study.
Underlying the recent maritime tensions in East Asia is a shifting of the
regional power balance from US primacy to China rising. The two
giants are seeking a new equilibrium of relationship both can eventually
accept.
State actors are advised to avoid overreactions to rivals’ outward toughness, which actually caters to the latter’s domestic audience. Heeding
this may advance the arrival of future regional stability.
